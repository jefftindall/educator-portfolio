Standards & Leadership

Tiffany brings standards work from committee rooms into mixed-ability Dance I classes every day. She assesses individual progress—not comparison between students—a perspective that supports clear, grade-band performance expectations.

Standards-writing experience

In 2008, she served on the Georgia Department of Education Performance Standards Writing Committee for Dance Education. She continues to design courses across Dance I–IV with sequenced objectives, 60/40 summative/formative grading, and self- and peer-assessment rubrics tied to the Georgia Standards of Excellence.

Artistic processes in the classroom

How anchor-standard processes look on a Tuesday in Dance class
Artistic process In Tiffany’s classroom Evidence
Creating Mixed-ability groups each choreograph a section of a song; sections are stitched into one class dance for the concert. Every student contributes and explains their choices. Block 3 student choreography video; Dance I–IV syllabi
Performing Every class performs at least two pieces per concert—one teacher-choreographed, one student-created. Concert programs; performance documentation
Responding Self- and peer-assessment rubrics; end-of-semester evaluations; Critical Response Process; field trips to professional performances (e.g., Alvin Ailey’s Revelations). CAN action plan; course syllabi
Connecting SEL competencies in improvisation and group work; kinesiology and injury prevention; cultural and social dance units. NDEO conference notes; classroom materials
Assessment philosophy (CAN journal): Measure each student’s growth against their own starting point—not against classmates with different training histories.

Leveling the playing field

Through CAN action research, Tiffany studied what happened when students with prior studio training dominated group work while others felt they did not belong. Her wondering: can SEL practices help every student feel valued regardless of training background? Pair/Think/Create structures and explicit peer teaching became central to how she implements Student Voice & Choice.

Postsecondary readiness

Structured choreography projects over multiple years teach collaboration and leadership. Former students have moved into Project SEARCH pathways, work-based learning as assistant teachers, university adjunct work, and professional training with scholarship support.

Technology & digital tools

Tiffany uses video review for self-assessment, recorded choreography for peer feedback, and accessibility supports so students can participate fully. As NCAS integrates technology and AI with artistic literacy, she approaches new tools the same way she approaches standards: keep expectations high, change the instructional method, and protect student voice and privacy.

Why I want to serve on the NCAS writing team

The field asked for refinement—not a rewrite: clearer language, less vagueness, better grade-band alignment, and more support through exemplars and professional development. Tiffany’s daily work is proof that Arts Learning for All and Concise Language are not abstract goals. She teaches general and special education students to the same standards in one room, then helps them perform together on one stage. She wants to bring that classroom lens—warm, practical, and story-led—to the standards themselves.
